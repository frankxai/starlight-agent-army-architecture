# Starlight eye-language calibration v1

Four actual 1122 × 1402 repo exports were inspected at high detail. These are optical-language concepts, not approved identity masters: every generated close-up drifted some Lyra face geometry while testing the intended eye system.

## Outcome

The current tiny cyan dots are too cold and under-articulated. Fully human eyes are too uncanny and imply biological experience. The strongest territory lies between them: visible gaze and pupil depth, medium-small rounded proportions, low luminance, and clearly engineered non-human construction.

## Recommended Starlight grammar

The likely winning system is an E01 × E02 synthesis:

- medium-small softly faceted aperture, approximately twelve percent smaller than E01;
- dark focus core and visibly layered depth from E01;
- quiet six-petal mechanical iris from E02;
- no sclera, skin, eyelashes, eyebrows, smile, projected emotion, or cyan glow;
- gaze direction carried by a movable focus core;
- neutral upper canopy, not an eyebrow;
- identity restored to the exact approved cranium and faceplate;
- role families may vary the housing, but not the ethical gaze rules.

## Why not simply use human eyes?

Human-readable gaze is useful; human-looking ocular anatomy is not necessary. Research supports subtle gaze behavior and suggests rounder eyes can improve first impressions in social robots, while trust remains driven by perceived competence and warmth rather than anthropomorphism alone. Other work finds that eyes may add only marginal trust and can reduce collaborative performance, and recent implicit measures still find an uncanny-valley response. Starlight should therefore borrow gaze legibility, not biological imitation.

## Role-based evolution

- Creator, Academy, community, and generalist conductors: E01 × E02 hybrid with the softest canopy and clearest gaze.
- Research, foresight, science, and knowledge agents: more E02 petal structure and visible focus mechanics.
- Build, protocol, spatial, operations, and device agents: E03-derived optical instruments or no humanoid face.
- Trust, safety, governance, finance, and health boundaries: smaller apertures, lower expressivity, explicit human gates, and no cuteness.
- Compact companions: larger apertures may be allowed only for low-stakes onboarding and celebration; they must never communicate authority.
- Biotech and ecological agents: organic optical materials may vary, but biological human-eye mimicry remains blocked.
