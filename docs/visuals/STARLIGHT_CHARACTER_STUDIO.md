# Starlight Character Studio

The Character Studio is the visual identity layer for Starlight agent systems. It makes an agent's specialty, operating style, and place in a swarm understandable at a glance while keeping every capability claim grounded in the canonical agent card and governed runtime.

It is not a costume generator, a runtime authority source, or a substitute for evaluation evidence.

## Design premise

The strongest current Starlight portraits already establish a recognizable dark porcelain-and-obsidian robot family. The next quality step is controlled differentiation: keep the family resemblance, improve material and anatomical craft, and let each role alter the silhouette, instrument, gesture, and handoff behavior.

For the Research Team pilot, Lyra remains recognizably Lyra while three bounded directions test how much functional morphology the system can add:

- **Porcelain Atelier** — the closest premium evolution of the current ceremonial portrait.
- **Precision Instrument** — the role becomes part of the machine's structure; strongest test of first-glance specialization.
- **Warm Companion** — quieter optics, softer shoulders, and attentive hands test trust without cuteness or human mimicry.

The comparison deliberately holds identity, crop, background density, material family, and emotional baseline constant.

## Visual grammar tied to work

| Role family | Reads as | Receives | Produces | Useful morphology |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relay | orchestration | bounded tasks and policy | routed task packets | channels, junction collar, open handoff gesture |
| Lens | research and observation | source fields | ranked signals | optical aperture, calibrated viewing instrument |
| Loom | synthesis | evidence threads | coherent narrative or model | interlacing seams, convergence frame |
| Prism | critique and comparison | claim or proposal | separated assumptions and risks | split planes, counter-angle instrument |
| Vault | memory and stewardship | approved records | retrieved context with provenance | protected compartments, seal and index cues |
| Measure | evaluation and governance | run evidence | scored verdict and escalation | gauge, balance, external approval marker |

These forms are metaphors for real inputs, transformations, and outputs—not implied superpowers. Cyan denotes machine activity. Amber denotes human authority and remains outside the robot body.

## System of record

```text
canonical agent card + source commit
                 |
                 v
      character visual contract
                 |
        +--------+--------+
        |        |        |
        v        v        v
    direction  direction  direction
      job A      job B      job C
        |        |        |
        +--------+--------+
                 |
       inspected candidate set
                 |
        selection receipt
                 |
          human decision
                 |
       approved identity master
                 |
     site / icon / motion derivatives
```

Every generated candidate retains its prompt, reference role and rights status, provider/lane, file hash, pixel dimensions, inspection notes, score, and next change. An approved contract must point to an explicit human approval. A visual identity never grants tools, memory, credentials, runtime admission, or permission to act.

## Resolution and derivative policy

- Generate the identity master as a large 4:5 PNG. Preserve the original generation artifact.
- Create lossless review crops before compressed production derivatives.
- Derive 4:5 cards, 1:1 directory portraits, 16:9 operational scenes, and 96-pixel role badges from the approved identity master.
- Keep public labels and infographics in real HTML/SVG overlays; do not ask an image model to render functional interface text.
- Test faceplate, silhouette, signature instrument, and activity/authority colors at the smallest production crop.
- Use motion to reveal routing, evidence flow, or human approval—not as ambient ornament.

## Quality and governance gates

1. The canonical card, graph, prompt contract, and capability boundary exist.
2. The visual contract contains immutable identity cues and explicitly prohibited claims.
3. At least two controlled candidates are generated; three is the default exploration set.
4. Actual exports are inspected and scored. 26/30 is the minimum for human review, not automatic publication.
5. The selection receipt records comparison and provenance.
6. A human approves the identity before any production integration.
7. Site implementation passes visual, accessibility, performance, and reduced-motion QA.

The schemas and reusable files live in `schemas/character-studio/` and `templates/starlight-character-studio/`. The initial Lyra pilot lives in `assets/starlight-constellation/v2-preview/research-team/` and remains a preview until Frank selects or blends a direction.
