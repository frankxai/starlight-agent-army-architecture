Why the old study did not settle it.

The earlier board changed openings on a nearly blank mask. It taught us what to avoid, but not how to integrate gaze, character identity, optical depth, and role. These remain useful controls rather than canon candidates.

Science translated into design

Six rules for likeable machine vision.

These are design inferences, not universal psychological laws. The research is contextual, so Starlight should test actual target users and avoid treating appearance as proof of trustworthiness.

01 · Gaze before glow

A dark focus core must visibly orient.

People need to know what the agent is attending to. A bright LED signals activity but not gaze.

02 · Moderate roundness

Rounder than hostile slits; smaller than mascots.

Use enough roundness for approachability without activating childlike authority cues.

03 · Optical depth

Socket, lens, iris, focus core.

Layered construction creates life-like attention through physics, not biological imitation.

04 · Honest materials

Smoked glass, ceramic, graphite, quiet light.

No wet sclera, skin, lashes, or features that imply a biological interior.

05 · Functional motion

Attention, thinking, uncertainty, handoff.

Move the focus core and aperture for operational states; do not simulate joy, sadness, or love as if felt.

06 · Role-weighted warmth

Social agents can be warmer; authority agents calmer.

Health, safety, finance, governance, and infrastructure must not borrow cuteness to gain compliance.

Civilization evolution

One ethical grammar, several optical families.

The civilization should evolve different sensor forms for different work. Consistency comes from the gaze rules, materials, luminance, and behavior—not from forcing 144 agents to wear the same face.
